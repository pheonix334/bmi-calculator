How it works

  1. Choose your units. Toggle between metric (kg/cm) or imperial (lb/in).
  2. Enter your height and weight. These are the only two numbers BMI needs.
  3. Get your result instantly. Your BMI is calculated as weight ÷ height², then matched to a category (underweight, normal, overweight, obese) and shown on the color-coded scale.
  4. See your healthy range. The result also shows the weight range considered "normal BMI" for your specific height.

BMI is a quick screening number based on height and weight alone — it doesn't account for muscle mass, bone density, or body composition. Use it as a starting point, not a full picture of your health.

BMI categories at a glance

CategoryBMI rangeGeneral guidance
UnderweightBelow 18.5Worth discussing with a doctor, especially with unintentional weight loss.
Normal weight18.5–24.9Associated with lower average health risk in most population studies.
Overweight25–29.9Worth monitoring, especially alongside other risk factors like waist size.
Obese30 and aboveHigher associated health risk — consider talking to a doctor about next steps.

Some health organizations, including several in Asia, use lower BMI cutoffs (e.g. 23 for overweight, 27.5 for obese) because health risk tends to appear at a lower BMI in some populations. If you're unsure which reference applies to you, ask your doctor.
